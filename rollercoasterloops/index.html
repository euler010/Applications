Coney Island's Circular Loop (1895)

In 1895, Lina Beecher introduced North America's first looping coaster, the Flip Flap Railway at Coney Island's Sea Lion Park. It utilized a true circular loop.

While modern physics textbooks popularized a claim of 12 Gs, that figure is physically unsupported by the ride's documented 40-to-45-foot structure. A mathematical analysis of the actual geometry yields an entry spike of roughly ~7.5 Gs. In an era of upright seating and rigid wooden cars without headrests, this instantaneous shock was more than enough to cause severe neck whiplash and blackouts, prompting engineers to shift to gradual, teardrop-shaped clothoid loops.

Flip Flap Force Peak: ~7.5 G (Injurious)

The True Clothoid (Euler Spiral)

An Euler Spiral is a curve where curvature increases linearly with its curve length.

Mathematically, its radius (R) is inversely proportional to the distance traveled (s). As the train enters the loop, the radius starts infinitely large (flat track) and smoothly shrinks. This geometric transition mirrors the train's deceleration perfectly, managing centripetal force and neutralizing G-force jolts.
